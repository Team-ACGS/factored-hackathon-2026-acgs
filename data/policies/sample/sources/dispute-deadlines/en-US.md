## Purpose

This policy explains how long the bank takes to handle a claim for a charge you do not recognize, from the moment you open it until the case is closed.
It brings together in one place the deadlines the bank commits to and the deadlines the rule sets, so you know what to expect at each stage.
It also explains what you can do if a deadline is missed or if you disagree with the decision.
The bank applies this policy to every claim in the same way, whatever the amount of the charge or the channel it came through.

## Scope

This policy applies to credit and debit card charges the cardholder does not recognize, whether they are in-store purchases, online purchases or recurring charges.
You can open a claim in {{policy.channels.app_clara}}, where Clara, the bank's virtual assistant, guides you step by step, or by phone at {{policy.channels.phone}}.
It does not apply to loans, accounts, investments or insurance, which have their own rules.
It also does not apply to a price difference with a merchant when you do recognize the purchase.

## Definitions

A claim is the request you make when you do not recognize a charge, and it moves through stages you can follow in the app.
Your case is {{policy.claims.stage_opened}} when the bank records the request, and it is {{policy.claims.stage_assigned}} when an analyst takes it.
While the analyst studies the evidence, your case is {{policy.claims.stage_in_review}}.
When the bank decides, your case is {{policy.claims.stage_resolved}}, and it is {{policy.claims.stage_closed}} when no further steps are possible.
A provisional credit is an amount the bank may give back while it reviews the case, without that being a decision yet.

## Principles

The bank handles every claim with the same care and tells you about each change of stage in plain words.
The bank never asks you to prove that you did not make the purchase: it reviews the records of the charge and decides based on them.
You keep your rights even if some time has passed since the purchase, as long as you report the charge within the deadline of this policy.
The bank does not promise an outcome before reviewing the case, and it always explains the reasons for its decision.

## Rules

You can report a charge you do not recognize up to {{policy.claims.report_window}} after the date of the statement it appears on.
The bank assigns your case to an analyst within {{policy.claims.assign_time}} of opening it.
If the analyst needs more information, they ask for it in the app, and you have {{policy.claims.evidence_window}} to send it.
If you do not send it, the bank decides with the information it has.
Each claim covers a single charge, although Clara can help you open several at once if you find more charges you do not recognize.

## Customer rights

You have the right to receive a case number and to follow each stage in {{policy.channels.app_claims}}.
You have the right to know the bank's decision and its reasons, in writing and in plain language.
If you disagree with the decision, you can ask the bank to reopen your case within {{policy.claims.reopen_window}} after it closes, with new information.
You can also turn to the bank's ombudsman office or to the consumer protection agency at any time, without losing your claim.

## Bank obligations

The bank reviews your case within {{policy.claims.review_time}} after it is {{policy.claims.stage_assigned}}, and commits to resolving it within {{policy.claims.resolution_time}} of opening it.
Where the policy allows it, the bank may apply a provisional credit within {{policy.claims.provisional_credit_time}} while the case is still in review.
That credit is not a decision and may be reversed if the review confirms the charge.
The bank lets you know in the app every time your case changes stage and keeps a record of every step.

## Exceptions

Some cases take longer because they depend on third parties, such as the card network or the merchant.
When that happens, the bank explains the reason and gives you the new estimated date before the original deadline ends.
A duplicate charge follows a shorter review, of {{policy.transactions.duplicate_review}}, because the bank compares the matching records directly.
If you recognize the charge during the review, you can ask the bank to close the case without a decision.

## Deadlines

The bank commits to resolving your claim within {{policy.claims.resolution_time}}, a deadline that never goes beyond the legal maximum.
The rule gives the bank up to {{policy.legal.claim_response}} to answer a complaint, and gives the customer up to {{policy.legal.report_window}} to dispute a charge, as the bank reads the applicable rule.
If the bank's deadline passes without a decision, the case is not closed: it stays open and you can escalate it right away.
Deadlines in business days do not count weekends or public holidays.

## Regulatory basis

The bank follows the rules of the banking regulator ({{policy.authority.regulator}}) and of the consumer protection agency ({{policy.authority.consumer_agency}}).
The rule that governs claims ({{policy.authority.norm_name}}) gives up to {{policy.legal.claim_response}} for an answer, as the bank reads the applicable rule.
If you are not satisfied with the answer, you can write to the bank's ombudsman office, {{policy.service.ombudsman_name}}, at {{policy.service.ombudsman}}, which answers within {{policy.service.ombudsman_response}}.
